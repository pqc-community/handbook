On June 6, 2025, the US President signed [Executive Order 14306](/standards/us-government-requirements/eo-14306/), which amends Executive Order 14144 of January 16, 2025, and replaces its section on post-quantum cryptography. The order is direction to US federal departments and agencies: it calls for a CISA list of product categories with post-quantum support and for requirements that agencies support TLS 1.3 by January 2, 2030.

## What the order says

EO 14306 amends Executive Order 13694 and Executive Order 14144. For post-quantum cryptography, the relevant change is a replacement Section 4(f) of EO 14144, which opens by stating that a cryptanalytically relevant quantum computer would be able to break much of the public-key cryptography in use, and that National Security Memorandum 10 of May 4, 2022, directed the Federal Government to prepare for the transition.

The new section sets two tasks, both due by December 1, 2025. The Secretary of Homeland Security, acting through the Director of the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) and in consultation with the Director of the National Security Agency (NSA), is to release, and then regularly update, a list of product categories in which products that support post-quantum cryptography are widely available. The Director of NSA for national security systems, and the Director of the Office of Management and Budget (OMB) for other federal systems, are each to issue requirements for agencies to support TLS 1.3 or a successor version as soon as practicable, and no later than January 2, 2030.

## Who it applies to

The order directs US federal departments and agencies, with national security systems handled by NSA rather than OMB. It does not itself bind private organizations, and the 2030 date reaches agencies through the requirements NSA and OMB are to issue rather than directly through the order. Suppliers to US federal agencies should still expect it to shape what those agencies ask for.

## What it means for your plan

The TLS 1.3 deadline is about protocol support for data in transit, which makes it a key-establishment matter; the [TLS explainer](/learn/explainers/tls/) covers how post-quantum key exchange fits into a TLS 1.3 connection. It does not move certificates or other signatures to post-quantum algorithms, so keep those on their own line in your plan.

If you are a US federal agency, record the January 2, 2030, date against your TLS endpoints and the services that terminate them, and watch for the NSA and OMB requirements due by December 1, 2025, which will set out how agencies are to meet it. [Set dates and milestones](/plan/program/dates/) shows how to record a date with its issuer and audience so it is not later read as a universal deadline; if you supply federal agencies, record it as your customers' date rather than your own.

For other organizations, the CISA list, once published, will be a useful reference when you [work with suppliers](/plan/program/suppliers/): a category in which post-quantum products are widely available is one where asking for post-quantum support is a reasonable purchase requirement. The order's dates themselves apply only to its federal audience.
