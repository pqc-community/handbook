On 18 September 2025 NIST published [SP 800-227, Recommendations for Key-Encapsulation Mechanisms](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/227/final), as a final Special Publication. It explains how to use key-encapsulation mechanisms (KEMs) such as ML-KEM securely, including the key combiners behind hybrid key establishment, and it is written for US federal agencies while serving as a reference for anyone implementing or buying post-quantum key establishment.

## What NIST published

A KEM is a set of algorithms that two parties can use to establish a shared secret key over a public channel, which can then protect data with symmetric-key encryption and authentication. [FIPS 203](/standards/post-quantum/fips-203/), published on 13 August 2024, specifies ML-KEM, the NIST-standardised post-quantum KEM, but a standard for an algorithm says little about how to use it inside a protocol or product. SP 800-227, summarised on the handbook's [SP 800-227 page](/standards/post-quantum/sp-800-227/), fills that gap: it sets out the basic definitions, security properties and applications of KEMs, and gives recommendations for handling keys and ciphertexts safely.

It also covers key combiners, which merge two or more shared secrets into a single key. That is the mechanism behind hybrid key establishment, where a classical exchange such as elliptic-curve Diffie–Hellman and ML-KEM each contribute a secret, so the result stays protected as long as either one holds.

The final version follows an initial public draft released on 7 January 2025, and the comments received on that draft are published alongside it.

## Recommendation, not mandate

SP 800-227 is a NIST recommendation addressed to US federal agencies. It does not itself require anything of anyone; requirements arise where a FIPS, a validation programme or a directive points to it. Outside the US federal government it carries weight as a reference that suppliers, certification bodies and other authorities can cite, rather than as an obligation.

## What it means for your plan

SP 800-227 is mainly for the people who build cryptography into products, so most organisations will meet it through their suppliers. When you ask suppliers about post-quantum key establishment, as [Work with suppliers](/plan/programme/suppliers/) describes, it gives you a published reference to ask them to name: whether their ML-KEM implementation and any hybrid combination follow NIST's recommendations, and whether the modules concerned are, or will be, validated.

If you build systems yourself, the document is the reference for the engineering team that implements key establishment, and [Hybrid cryptography](/learn/hybrid/) explains why a hybrid combination is common during the transition. Keep it to key establishment: SP 800-227 says nothing about digital signatures, which follow a separate path with different drivers.
