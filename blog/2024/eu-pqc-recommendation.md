On April 11, 2024, the European Commission adopted [Recommendation (EU) 2024/1101](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reco/2024/1101/oj) on a coordinated implementation roadmap for the transition to post-quantum cryptography (PQC). It is addressed to EU Member States and concerns the digital infrastructure and services of public administrations and other critical infrastructure. It asks Member States to agree a joint roadmap within two years, and it does not itself bind organizations.

{/* truncate */}

## What the Commission adopted

The Recommendation sets out to foster the transition to post-quantum cryptography for public administrations and other critical infrastructure in the Union. It recommends that Member States coordinate through a dedicated forum, a new sub-group of the NIS Cooperation Group, which could include national security agencies, national cybersecurity authorities and the EU Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA), and which may invite industry, service providers and operators to contribute.

Member States are invited to set up that sub-group soon after publication and to appoint experts to define and develop the Post-Quantum Cryptography Coordinated Implementation Roadmap. The Recommendation expects the roadmap to be available two years after publication, followed by national transition plans that develop or align with it. In its recitals, the Commission says the roadmap should list the actions for Member States, with a clear timeline for phases and milestones, and that Member States should consider migrating their public administration and critical infrastructure systems as soon as possible.

## Recommendation, not law

A Commission Recommendation is not binding law. This one is adopted under Article 292 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, and its wording is that of encouragement: Member States are "encouraged" and "invited", not required. The Commission says it will monitor progress with Member States' experts and assess whether further steps are needed, including proposing binding acts of Union law, with a review of the Recommendation's effects within three years of publication.

The Recommendation sets no migration dates of its own. Any milestones will come from the roadmap the sub-group develops, and they will be addressed to Member States first, not directly to individual organizations.

## What it means for your plan

If you supply or operate systems for public administrations or critical infrastructure in the EU, expect national roadmaps and procurement requirements to follow from this work, and record the Recommendation in your plan as an EU signal with no dates yet. [Set dates and milestones](/plan/program/dates/) shows how to note a source like this so its status is clear, and [Why migration timelines differ](/learn/explainers/why-timelines-differ/) explains why an EU roadmap will not set a universal deadline.

Nothing here changes the first steps. Organizations outside that scope can carry on with the [core actions](/plan/core-actions/), and those with a program can keep building their [cryptographic inventory](/plan/program/inventory/), which any later roadmap will need. Key establishment and signatures will still need separate treatment, because their drivers differ, as the [quantum threat lesson](/learn/quantum-threat/) explains.
