On 10 November 2026 the Dutch government's Rijksbrede Quantumstrategie, its government-wide quantum strategy, was published as an annex to a letter to the House of Representatives. It includes a PQC migration timeline for central government organisations that follows the [EU PQC roadmap](/standards/migration-guides/eu-nis-roadmap/), with milestones at the end of 2027, 2030 and 2035, and applies to central government rather than to Dutch organisations generally.

{/* truncate */}

## What the strategy says

The [strategy](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/blg-1279064.pdf), published in Dutch, says the EU roadmap describes a timeline to 2035 with interim goals and that the Dutch central government aims to keep to it. Its timeline for central government organisations asks for a draft PQC migration plan, with pilots prepared or under way for high- and medium-risk use cases, by the end of 2027; migration of high-risk use cases completed, and software and firmware using quantum-safe cryptography by default, by the end of 2030; and migration of medium-risk use cases completed by the end of 2035, with low-risk use cases completed as far as possible.

The strategy says the timeline can be adjusted or accelerated as the risk and threat picture changes, and that central government organisations may migrate faster where their risk appetite calls for it. It is a government strategy for its own organisations, not a regulation for businesses.

## What it means for your plan

For Dutch central government bodies, these are now their government's own milestones, so record them on [Set dates and milestones](/plan/programme/dates/) with their source and audience. The end-2030 point for high-risk use cases matches the EU roadmap, which bases its risk levels on the quantum risk score in the [Dutch PQC Migration Handbook](/standards/migration-guides/dutch-pqc-handbook/); use that score, or the handbook's [Decide what to migrate first](/plan/programme/priority/) practice, to decide which of your use cases are high-risk.

Organisations that supply Dutch central government should expect these dates to reach procurement, since the strategy also expects government organisations to be able to set requirements on quantum-safe cryptography when buying.
