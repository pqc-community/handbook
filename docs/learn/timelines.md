A published standard names an algorithm. A timeline says when an audience is expected to move. Those are different claims.

[FIPS 203](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/203/final), [FIPS 204](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/204/final), and [FIPS 205](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/205/final) are final standards. Final does not give every organisation the same date, and it does not put key establishment and signatures on one date.

[NIST IR 8547](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.IR.8547), October 2026, explains that harvest-now, decrypt-later applies where a public-key scheme establishes symmetric keys that protect confidentiality, whereas an authentication system remains secure until the signature algorithm can be broken at the time of verification — placing the two jobs at different points in a transition. The [UK NCSC timelines](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/pqc-migration-timelines), published and reviewed 20 March 2025, likewise separate standard browsers, operating systems, and mobile devices onto a supplier-update path, while custom or specialised software requires active planning.

Record the timeline that applies where the organisation operates and where it sells. A date in one report is that report's date for its audience. The rows, including who each date is for, are on [Whose date applies](/learn/explainers/timelines/).
