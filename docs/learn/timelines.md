A cryptographic standard answers what an algorithm or scheme specifies. A migration timeline describes when a particular audience is expected or recommended to take action. A standard's publication date does not set a migration date for every organisation.

Dates in PQC guidance can mark different events: planning, discovery, the start or completion of migration, or the deprecation and disallowance of algorithms under a standard. The verb matters. “Complete by” and “begin by” describe different milestones, even when they appear in documents about the same transition.

The source matters too. A standards body can set a transition schedule for use under its standards. A government directive can impose requirements on named agencies. A national cyber authority can recommend target dates for organisations in its stated audience. These instruments do not have the same authority or scope.

Key establishment and digital signatures can also have different migration timelines. Harvest-now, decrypt-later makes the confidentiality lifetime of data relevant to key establishment. Signatures protect authenticity and integrity, so their timing depends on how and when systems verify them. NIST describes these as separate parts of the transition in [IR 8547](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.IR.8547).

The [timeline comparison](/learn/explainers/timelines/) shows how these differences appear in NIST, US federal, UK, EU, and Australian guidance. For the distinction between a published algorithm standard and a product or protocol ready to use it, see [Standardisation](/learn/standardisation/).
