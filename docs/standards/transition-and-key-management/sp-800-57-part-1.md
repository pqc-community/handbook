## What it covers

NIST Special Publication (SP) 800-57 Part 1 gives general key-management guidance: how keys are generated, distributed, stored, used, backed up, archived and destroyed; the types of keys and other keying material; cryptoperiods; and comparable security strengths across symmetric and public-key algorithms. It is the background reference for questions such as how long a key should be used and what strength a scheme provides.

NIST issues SPs as guidance and recommendations for US federal agencies; others widely use them as a reference. The current version is Revision 5, published on 4 May 2020. NIST released an initial public draft of Revision 6 on 5 December 2025, and although its comment period closed on 5 February 2026, it is still a draft.

## Relationship with other standards

[NIST IR 8547](/standards/transition-and-key-management/nist-ir-8547/) notes that SP 800-57 Part 1 had projected that NIST would disallow public-key schemes providing 112 bits of security on 1 January 2031. Because migration to quantum-resistant algorithms is needed in the same period, NIST will instead deprecate all quantum-vulnerable digital signature algorithms after 2030 and disallow them under NIST standards and guidelines after 2035 (§4.1.1). IR 8547 applies the same 2030 and 2035 dates to quantum-vulnerable key-establishment schemes (§4.1.2). For US federal high value assets and high-impact systems, earlier deadlines under [EO 14412](/standards/us-government-requirements/eo-14412/) and [OMB M-26-15](/standards/us-government-requirements/omb-m-26-15/) constrain continued use.

Formal approval status for algorithms and key lengths is carried in [SP 800-131A](/standards/transition-and-key-management/sp-800-131a/). SP 800-57 Part 1 remains useful for the key-lifecycle side of a migration: knowing which keys exist, how long they live and when they are due for replacement feeds directly into an [inventory](/plan/programme/inventory/).

The Rev. 6 draft would bring the document into line with the post-quantum standards: NIST's announcement says it adds the algorithms in [FIPS 203](/standards/post-quantum/fips-203/), [FIPS 204](/standards/post-quantum/fips-204/) and [FIPS 205](/standards/post-quantum/fips-205/), Ascon from [SP 800-232](/standards/symmetric-and-hash/sp-800-232/) and the post-quantum security categories, and replaces the document's own time frames for algorithm approval status with references to SP 800-131A. Until it is published as final, Rev. 5 remains the current text.
