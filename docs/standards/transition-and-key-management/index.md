These NIST publications explain when quantum-vulnerable algorithms leave NIST standards, how NIST records changes in algorithm approval status, how keys should be managed through their lifecycle, and how organisations can build the agility to change algorithms again. None of them is binding on its own: NIST SPs are guidance and recommendations for US federal agencies, and NIST IRs and white papers are informative. Binding US federal deadlines come from directives such as [EO 14412](/standards/us-federal-directives/eo-14412/) and [OMB M-26-15](/standards/us-federal-directives/omb-m-26-15/).

## Which document do I need?

- When will NIST stop allowing RSA, ECDSA, EdDSA and Diffie-Hellman? See [NIST IR 8547](/standards/transition-and-key-management/nist-ir-8547/).
- What is the formal approval status of an algorithm or key length? See [NIST SP 800-131A](/standards/transition-and-key-management/sp-800-131a/).
- How should keys, cryptoperiods and security strengths be managed? See [NIST SP 800-57 Part 1](/standards/transition-and-key-management/sp-800-57-part-1/).
- How do we make it easier to change algorithms again? See [NIST CSWP 39](/standards/transition-and-key-management/cswp-39/).

### Transition schedule and approval status

| Publication | Summary | Reference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NIST IR 8547 | Final, 27 October 2026. NIST's schedule: quantum-vulnerable public-key algorithms deprecated after 2030 and disallowed under NIST standards and guidelines after 2035; also records earlier US federal deadlines. | [NIST IR 8547](/standards/transition-and-key-management/nist-ir-8547/) |
| NIST SP 800-131A | Rev. 2 final, 21 March 2019; Rev. 3 initial public draft 21 October 2024, still a draft. Records approval status of algorithms and key lengths. | [NIST SP 800-131A](/standards/transition-and-key-management/sp-800-131a/) |

| Publication | Summary | Reference |
| --- | --- | --- |
