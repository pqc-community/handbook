## What it covers

NIST Special Publication (SP) 800-131A is where NIST records the approval status of cryptographic algorithms and key lengths specified in FIPS and NIST SPs, and the dates on which that status changes. It uses four terms: acceptable, deprecated (usable, but the data owner must weigh the risk), legacy use (only to process already-protected information, such as decrypting or verifying) and disallowed.

NIST issues SPs as guidance and recommendations for US federal agencies; others widely use them as a reference. NIST IR 8547 describes SP 800-131A as addressing the transitions associated with the use of cryptography by federal agencies to protect controlled unclassified information (§4.1). Revision 2 is the current final version; Revision 3 remains an initial public draft.

## Relationship with other standards

[NIST IR 8547](/standards/transition-and-key-management/nist-ir-8547/), final October 2026, says NIST will revise SP 800-131A with more detailed guidelines and schedules during the transition to post-quantum cryptography (§4.1), and lists the Rev. 3 initial public draft in its references. IR 8547 sets the headline schedule: quantum-vulnerable public-key signatures and key-establishment schemes are deprecated after 2030 and disallowed under NIST standards and guidelines after 2035. It also says the few NIST symmetric standards at the 112-bit security level will be disallowed in 2030, and points to SP 800-131A for that change (§4.1.3).

SP 800-131A states status by algorithm and key length; it does not tell an organization which systems to migrate first. Earlier US federal deadlines for high value assets and high-impact systems come from [EO 14412](/standards/us-government-requirements/eo-14412/) and [OMB M-26-15](/standards/us-government-requirements/omb-m-26-15/), not from SP 800-131A. For key-management context and the earlier 112-bit projection, see [SP 800-57 Part 1](/standards/transition-and-key-management/sp-800-57-part-1/).
