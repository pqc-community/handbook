## What it covers

Executive Order 14412, Securing the Nation Against Advanced Cryptographic Attacks, sets out how US federal agencies are to move to post-quantum cryptography and assigns work to OMB, NIST, CISA, NSA and the Federal Acquisition Regulatory (FAR) Council. Its main provisions are:

- each agency is to designate a lead for post-quantum migration within 30 days;
- OMB is to issue guidance within 90 days requiring high-value assets and high-impact systems to use post-quantum key establishment by 31 December 2030 and post-quantum digital signatures by 31 December 2031 (OMB did so in [M-26-15](/standards/us-federal-directives/omb-m-26-15/));
- NIST is to run a pilot to be completed by 31 December 2027;
- NSA is to report on national security systems;
- CISA and NIST are to publish guidance on the minimum elements of a cryptographic bill of materials (CBOM) within 270 days;
- NIST is to revise Cryptographic Module Validation Program (CMVP) processes within 180 days to speed validation of post-quantum modules;
- the FAR Council is to propose, within 180 days, a rule requiring federal contractors to comply with NIST FIPS, including post-quantum cryptography, by 31 December 2030;
- the FAR Council is to make a rule, within 270 days, requiring contractors to run vulnerability disclosure programmes that accept reports of cryptographic weaknesses.

The President signed EO 14412 on 22 June 2026 and it was published in the Federal Register on 25 June 2026 (91 FR 38483). It is presidential direction to US federal agencies; national security systems are covered through NSA. It does not itself place obligations on contractors: the 2030 contractor requirement exists only as a rule the order tells the FAR Council to propose, and it binds contractors only if and when a final FAR rule takes effect. Critical infrastructure owners and operators are engaged through Sector Risk Management Agencies and CISA rather than bound directly.

## Relationship with other standards

EO 14412 builds on [NSM-10](/standards/us-federal-directives/nsm-10/) and [EO 14306](/standards/us-federal-directives/eo-14306/), and is carried out for agencies by [OMB M-26-15](/standards/us-federal-directives/omb-m-26-15/). The order gives key establishment (2030) and signatures (2031) separate dates. Their drivers differ: traffic recorded today can be decrypted later, whereas a forged signature becomes possible only once a capable quantum computer exists. Long-lived roots of trust and verifiers that cannot easily be updated need planning well before the signature date; see [Long-lived roots of trust](/technology-options/long-lived-roots/).

[NIST IR 8547 (PDF)](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.IR.8547), October 2026, was issued in support of EO 14412 and notes that the order directs NIST to revise CMVP processes for [FIPS 140-3](/standards/modules-protocols-identity/fips-140-3/) validation of post-quantum modules. The CBOM guidance the order calls for relates to [inventory work](/migration-practices/inventory/); suppliers to the US government should watch the FAR rulemaking rather than treat 2030 as a contract term already in force.
