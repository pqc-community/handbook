## What it covers

Executive Order 14306 amends Executive Order 13694 and Executive Order 14144. For post-quantum cryptography, its important change is a replacement Section 4(f) of EO 14144. That section directs CISA, working with the National Security Agency (NSA), to publish by 1 December 2025 a list of product categories in which products supporting post-quantum cryptography are widely available; directs NSA (for national security systems) and OMB (for other federal systems) to issue requirements by 1 December 2025; and requires agencies to support TLS 1.3 or a successor version no later than 2 January 2030.

The President signed EO 14306 on 6 June 2025 and it was published in the Federal Register on 11 June 2025 (90 FR 24723). It is presidential direction to US federal departments and agencies, with national security systems handled by NSA rather than OMB. It does not directly bind private organisations, although the CISA list it called for is useful to any buyer.

## Relationship with other standards

The product-category list EO 14306 called for is summarised at [CISA PQC product categories](/standards/us-federal-directives/cisa-pqc-product-categories/). [OMB M-26-15](/standards/us-federal-directives/omb-m-26-15/) refers agencies to that list and repeats the TLS 1.3 deadline in its Appendix A. [EO 14412](/standards/us-federal-directives/eo-14412/), June 2026, then set firmer migration deadlines.

[NIST IR 8547 (PDF)](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.IR.8547), October 2026, notes that EO 14306 and M-26-15 require TLS 1.3 support by 2 January 2030 and treats TLS as an early priority, because TLS 1.3 is the near-term path to quantum-resistant key establishment, including hybrid key exchange. The TLS deadline is about key establishment in transit; it does not by itself move certificates or other signatures to post-quantum algorithms. See [NIST SP 800-52](/standards/modules-protocols-identity/sp-800-52/) and the [TLS explainer](/learn/explainers/tls/).
