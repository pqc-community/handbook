## What it covers

FIPS 140-3 sets the security requirements for cryptographic modules — the hardware, software and firmware components that implement approved algorithms. It defines four increasing security levels and covers matters such as the module boundary, approved security functions, physical security, key management and self-tests. Modules are tested against it under the Cryptographic Module Validation Program (CMVP), which NIST runs jointly with the Canadian Centre for Cyber Security; algorithm implementations are tested separately under the Cryptographic Algorithm Validation Program (CAVP).

FIPS 140-3 is issued by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST). NIST's FIPS compliance FAQ describes FIPS as standards for federal computer systems that are "not always mandatory for federal agencies": the applicability section of each FIPS says when it is mandatory, FIPS do not apply to national security systems, and private-sector organisations use them voluntarily, although companies with government contracts must comply with FISMA, which mandates the use of FIPS.

## Relationship with other standards

FIPS 140-3 is where the algorithm standards meet products. A module can only claim post-quantum algorithms such as ML-KEM ([FIPS 203](/standards/post-quantum/fips-203/)), ML-DSA ([FIPS 204](/standards/post-quantum/fips-204/)) and SLH-DSA ([FIPS 205](/standards/post-quantum/fips-205/)) as approved once their implementations have passed CAVP testing and the module has been validated. [NIST IR 8547](/standards/transition-and-key-management/nist-ir-8547/) (October 2026) says there will be an extended period in which quantum-vulnerable algorithms and their post-quantum replacements coexist and are validated by NIST, and that, as directed by [EO 14412](/standards/us-federal-directives/eo-14412/), NIST is revising CMVP processes to accelerate validation of modules implementing post-quantum algorithms.

IR 8547 also says FIPS 140 validation accommodates a hybrid key-establishment mode and dual signatures when suitably combined with a NIST-approved scheme. For key establishment, a combination that includes a shared secret from an approved scheme can be FIPS 140-validated even if other inputs are disallowed, with combiner guidance in [SP 800-227](/standards/post-quantum/sp-800-227/). For signatures, dual or composite schemes are accommodated if at least one component is approved. Buyers should ask suppliers for the specific validation certificates that cover the post-quantum algorithms they claim, rather than relying on a general FIPS 140 statement.
