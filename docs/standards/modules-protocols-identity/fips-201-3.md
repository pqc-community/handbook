## What it covers

FIPS 201-3 specifies the Personal Identity Verification (PIV) system used by US federal agencies for their employees and contractors: identity proofing, credential issuance and maintenance, and how PIV credentials are used for physical and logical access. PIV credentials rely on public-key certificates held on smart cards and on the readers, middleware and certification authorities that support them, so they depend on both digital signatures and key establishment.

FIPS 201-3 is issued by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) for US federal departments and agencies. NIST's FIPS compliance FAQ notes that each FIPS's applicability section says when it is mandatory and that FIPS do not apply to national security systems. Organisations outside the federal government are not bound by it, though suppliers of PIV products and services build to it.

## Relationship with other standards

[NIST IR 8547](/standards/transition-and-key-management/nist-ir-8547/) (October 2026) cites FIPS 201-3 as critical trust infrastructure that will require significant and sustained effort to migrate in a timely way, and notes that [OMB M-26-15](/standards/us-federal-directives/omb-m-26-15/) directs attention to modernising FICAM capabilities in support of post-quantum cryptography. Because credentials, cards and readers are replaced over multi-year cycles, the signature algorithms they verify behave like long-lived verifiers: a reader or card that cannot be updated in the field fixes the algorithms it accepts.

The post-quantum signature standards ([FIPS 204](/standards/post-quantum/fips-204/), [FIPS 205](/standards/post-quantum/fips-205/)) and their X.509 profiles, such as [RFC 9881](/standards/modules-protocols-identity/rfc-9881/) for ML-DSA, are the building blocks a future PIV revision would draw on. FIPS 201-3 itself does not yet specify post-quantum algorithms.
