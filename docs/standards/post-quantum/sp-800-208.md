## What it covers

NIST Special Publication (SP) 800-208 specifies two families of stateful hash-based signature schemes: the Leighton-Micali Signature (LMS) system with its multi-tree Hierarchical Signature System (HSS), and the eXtended Merkle Signature Scheme (XMSS) with its multi-tree variant (XMSS^MT). Each private key holds a finite pool of one-time signing keys, and the signer must ensure that no one-time key is ever used twice, so signing state has to be tracked reliably.

SP 800-208 is a recommendation issued by NIST for US federal agencies and is widely used as a reference by others. It does not itself mandate anything; requirements arise where a FIPS, a validation program or a directive points to it.

## Relationship with other standards

[NIST IR 8547](/standards/transition-and-key-management/nist-ir-8547/) (October 2026) describes the SP 800-208 schemes as resistant to quantum attack, but says that because they need strict operational controls to track signature indices across distributed systems, they are not intended for general use, though they may be useful for applications such as software and firmware signing (§2.1.1). Where state cannot be managed safely, the stateless schemes in [FIPS 204](/standards/post-quantum/fips-204/) and [FIPS 205](/standards/post-quantum/fips-205/) avoid this burden.

For US national security systems, the NSA's [CNSA 2.0](/standards/national-algorithm-positions/cnsa-2-0/) FAQ (version 2.2, October 2026) allows LMS and XMSS for signing firmware and software. Formats are defined by [RFC 9708](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9708) (HSS/LMS in CMS, January 2025) and [RFC 9802](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9802) (HSS and XMSS in X.509, June 2025). Firmware verifiers that cannot be updated after deployment raise their own planning questions; see [Firmware and secure boot](/technology-options/firmware-secure-boot/).
