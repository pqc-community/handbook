## What it covers

NIST Special Publication (SP) 800-227 provides implementation and operational guidance for key-encapsulation mechanisms (KEMs), such as ML-KEM in [FIPS 203](/standards/post-quantum/fips-203/). It covers the security properties of KEMs, safe handling of keys and ciphertexts, and key combiners that merge two or more shared secret keys into a single combined key — the mechanism behind hybrid key establishment.

SP 800-227 is a recommendation issued by NIST for US federal agencies and is widely used as a reference by others. It does not itself mandate anything; requirements arise where a FIPS, a validation programme or a directive points to it.

## Relationship with other standards

[NIST IR 8547](/standards/transition-and-key-management/nist-ir-8547/) (October 2026) points to SP 800-227 for guidance on key combiners (§3.2.1). IR 8547 describes hybrid key establishment as typically temporary, leading to a second transition, and states that as long as at least one input to an approved composite technique comes from an approved key-establishment scheme, the combination can be FIPS 140-validated even if other inputs are disallowed (§4.1.2). IR 8547 also identifies the generic composite construction in [SP 800-56C](/standards/symmetric-and-hash/sp-800-56c/), which concatenates the shared secrets before key derivation.

SP 800-227 concerns key establishment only. Hybrid TLS key exchange is defined for TLS 1.3 in [RFC 10024](/standards/modules-protocols-certificates/rfc-10024/); dual or composite signatures are a separate question addressed under [FIPS 204](/standards/post-quantum/fips-204/).
