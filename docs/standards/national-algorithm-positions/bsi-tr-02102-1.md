## What it covers

BSI TR-02102-1 is a Technical Guideline from Germany's Federal Office for Information Security (BSI) that lists the cryptographic mechanisms and key lengths the BSI recommends, covering asymmetric encryption and key agreement, symmetric encryption, hash functions, data authentication, instance authentication, secret sharing and random number generation. Version 2026-01, dated 23 January 2026, updates its post-quantum recommendations, adds migration periods and ends the recommendation of classical asymmetric mechanisms used alone.

The guideline states that it addresses primarily, and in a recommendatory manner, developers planning to introduce new cryptographic systems from 2026 onwards. It also states that its recommendations do not anticipate decisions such as those made in governmental evaluation and approval processes, and that certain other BSI documents, unlike this guideline, are binding for particular applications. Read it as BSI recommendations, not as a requirement on organisations in general.

## Relationship with other standards

For key establishment, the guideline recommends the quantum-safe KEMs FrodoKEM, Classic McEliece and ML-KEM-768 or ML-KEM-1024, but only in hybrid form, combined with a classical mechanism using a combiner such as CatKDF or KeyCombine. This differs from the NIST position, under which ML-KEM ([FIPS 203](/standards/post-quantum/fips-203/)) is approved on its own and [NIST IR 8547](/standards/transition-and-key-management/nist-ir-8547/) treats hybrids as typically temporary, and from the NSA's [CNSA 2.0](/standards/national-algorithm-positions/cnsa-2-0/), which does not require hybrids. [NIST SP 800-227](/standards/post-quantum/sp-800-227/) covers KEM combiners from the NIST side.

Version 2026-01 still lists Classic McEliece, but the BSI's notes of 29 September 2026 say that, after recent progress in cryptanalysis, Classic McEliece should currently no longer be used in new developments or when planning new cryptographic applications, and that FrodoKEM or ML-KEM (or HQC, once its standard is published) should be used for key agreement instead. The BSI says the results so far do not allow a practical attack on the parameter sets the guideline recommends, that a hybrid combination with Classic McEliece still gives at least the security of the classical scheme, and that FrodoKEM and ML-KEM are not affected. It will revise the recommendation in the next version of the guideline, expected in early 2027, and its end-2031 date for completing the move to post-quantum key agreement is unchanged.

For digital signatures, the guideline recommends post-quantum signatures only together with a classical signature, and recommends classical signatures until the end of 2035. It strongly recommends that products expected to remain in use beyond 2030 can be upgraded to quantum-safe methods, with software and firmware update mechanisms including post-quantum signatures. The hash-based schemes SLH-DSA ([FIPS 205](/standards/post-quantum/fips-205/)), XMSS and LMS ([NIST SP 800-208](/standards/post-quantum/sp-800-208/)) can in principle also be used alone, provided implementation security is carefully considered; the stateful XMSS and LMS are recommended only where secure state management can be guaranteed, for example when signing firmware updates. That matters for long-lived verifiers such as boot and update roots.

Protocol-specific recommendations for TLS, IPsec and SSH are published separately in TR-02102-2, TR-02102-3 and TR-02102-4. The end-2030 and end-2035 dates align with the [EU NIS Cooperation Group roadmap](/standards/migration-guides/eu-nis-roadmap/), which Germany's national guideline of October 2026 from the BMI, BMDS and BSI adopts for German administrations and organisations, as the EU roadmap summary describes.
