## What it covers

RFC 9881 specifies the conventions for using ML-DSA, the lattice-based signature algorithm from FIPS 204, in X.509 certificates and certificate revocation lists (CRLs). It defines the algorithm identifiers and the encodings for ML-DSA public keys, private keys and signatures, so that certification authorities, relying parties and PKI software can issue and verify post-quantum certificates consistently.

RFC 9881 was published by the Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF) in October 2025 as a Proposed Standard on the Standards Track. IETF RFCs are voluntary technical specifications: RFC 9881 binds no organization by itself, but certificate policies, products and sector rules that adopt ML-DSA in X.509 rely on it for interoperability.

## Relationship with other standards

RFC 9881 is the X.509 profile for [FIPS 204](/standards/post-quantum/fips-204/). Sibling RFCs cover the other NIST post-quantum algorithms in X.509: [RFC 9909](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9909) for SLH-DSA ([FIPS 205](/standards/post-quantum/fips-205/), December 2025), [RFC 9935](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9935) for ML-KEM public keys ([FIPS 203](/standards/post-quantum/fips-203/), March 2026), and [RFC 9802](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9802) for the stateful hash-based schemes HSS and XMSS ([SP 800-208](/standards/post-quantum/sp-800-208/), June 2025). [RFC 10073](/standards/modules-protocols-certificates/rfc-10073/) defines composite certificates that pair ML-DSA with a classical algorithm.

Certificates are a signature problem, distinct from key establishment, and moving a PKI to ML-DSA involves certification authorities, relying-party software and any devices that verify certificate chains, including long-lived verifiers that cannot easily be updated. See [PKI and certificates](/technology-options/pki/) and [Signatures and trust](/learn/signatures-and-trust/).
