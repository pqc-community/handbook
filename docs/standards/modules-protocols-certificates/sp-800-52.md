## What it covers

NIST SP 800-52 Rev. 2 gives guidance on selecting, configuring and using Transport Layer Security (TLS) implementations: which protocol versions to support (TLS 1.2 and TLS 1.3), which cipher suites and extensions to enable, and how server and client certificates should be handled. The current revision predates the post-quantum standards and does not cover post-quantum key establishment.

SP 800-52 is a NIST Special Publication: guidance NIST issues for US federal agencies, and widely used as a reference by others. It is not itself a mandatory standard outside the federal context, and other authorities publish their own TLS guidance.

## Relationship with other standards

[NIST IR 8547](/standards/transition-and-key-management/nist-ir-8547/) (October 2026) describes TLS as an early priority for migration, because it protects data in transit for nearly every federal system and so is a primary target for harvest-now, decrypt-later collection. It says TLS 1.3 provides a practical near-term path to quantum-resistant key establishment, including hybrid key-exchange mechanisms that combine ML-KEM ([FIPS 203](/standards/post-quantum/fips-203/)) with classical algorithms, and that guidance on this will be reflected in the next revision of SP 800-52. The IETF specification for those hybrid groups is [RFC 10024](/standards/modules-protocols-certificates/rfc-10024/).

For US federal agencies, [EO 14306](/standards/us-government-requirements/eo-14306/) and [OMB M-26-15](/standards/us-government-requirements/omb-m-26-15/) require support for TLS 1.3 no later than 2 January 2030. That deadline applies to those agencies, not to every organisation. Outside the US federal context, other authorities give their own advice; for example, the UK National Cyber Security Centre publishes "Using TLS to protect data" (15 October 2026). Post-quantum key exchange in TLS addresses confidentiality; certificate signatures in TLS are a separate migration, covered under [PKI and certificates](/technology-options/pki/).
