## What it covers

NIST SP 800-108 specifies key-derivation functions in counter, feedback and double-pipeline modes that use a pseudorandom function, such as HMAC, CMAC or KMAC, to derive further symmetric keys from a key that is already established. It applies once a key exists, for example after key establishment and derivation under [NIST SP 800-56C](/standards/symmetric-and-primitives/sp-800-56c/).

NIST issues Special Publications in the 800 series as guidance and recommendations for US federal agencies; they are widely used as a reference by others. An SP is not itself mandatory unless a FIPS, directive or contract makes it so.

## Relationship with other standards

[NIST IR 8547](/standards/transition-and-key-management/nist-ir-8547/) (October 2026) cites SP 800-108 among NIST's key-derivation standards and says NIST does not expect to need to transition away from its symmetric standards as part of the post-quantum migration. The planned Rev. 2 is a revision of the publication, not a post-quantum replacement.

The post-quantum change happens upstream, in how the initial key is established ([FIPS 203](/standards/post-quantum/fips-203/), [NIST SP 800-227](/standards/post-quantum/sp-800-227/)). The pseudorandom functions it relies on are in [FIPS 198-1](/standards/symmetric-and-primitives/fips-198-1/) and [NIST SP 800-185](/standards/symmetric-and-primitives/sp-800-185/).
