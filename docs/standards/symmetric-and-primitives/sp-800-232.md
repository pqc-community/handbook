## What it covers

NIST SP 800-232 specifies the Ascon family of lightweight symmetric algorithms for resource-constrained devices: Ascon-AEAD128 for authenticated encryption with associated data, Ascon-Hash256 for hashing, and Ascon-XOF128 and Ascon-CXOF128 as extendable-output functions. It is a symmetric standard, not one of the post-quantum public-key standards.

NIST issues Special Publications in the 800 series as guidance and recommendations for US federal agencies; they are widely used as a reference by others. An SP is not itself mandatory unless a FIPS, directive or contract makes it so.

## Relationship with other standards

[NIST IR 8547](/standards/transition-and-key-management/nist-ir-8547/) (October 2026) lists SP 800-232 among the standards that are significantly less vulnerable to known quantum attacks (§4.1.3). Its Tables 7 and 8 place Ascon-AEAD128 in post-quantum security category 1, and Ascon-Hash256, Ascon-XOF128 and Ascon-CXOF128 in category 2 (for the XOFs, assuming a large enough output size).

For constrained devices, the post-quantum work is therefore in key establishment and signature verification, not in the Ascon primitives. Where such a device verifies firmware signatures with a key it cannot update, plan that verifier separately; see [Firmware and secure boot](/technology-options/firmware-secure-boot/). For general-purpose systems, AES in [FIPS 197](/standards/symmetric-and-primitives/fips-197/) and hashing in [FIPS 180-4](/standards/symmetric-and-primitives/fips-180-4/) and [FIPS 202](/standards/symmetric-and-primitives/fips-202/) remain the usual choices.
