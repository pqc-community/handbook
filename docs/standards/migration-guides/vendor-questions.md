## What it covers

This publication gives buyers practical questions for assessing a vendor's post-quantum cryptography readiness during procurement, contract renewal and ongoing vendor assurance: which public-key cryptography a product uses, how standards-based post-quantum updates will be delivered and validated, whether cryptographic agility is supported, and whether embedded or hardware-bound keys restrict field updates. The questions follow the phases of ASD's LATICE framework: locate and inventory cryptographic dependencies, assess risk, triage and prioritize, implement, and communicate and educate. They ask vendors for cryptographic bills of materials (CBOMs) or equivalent inventories, say that any post-quantum/traditional hybrid should be a clearly defined, temporary transition measure, and ask whether post-quantum-ready releases will arrive early enough for customers to test and roll them out before the end of 2030. The guide says that date reflects ASD's own transition milestone and tells readers to align with the guidance that applies to their jurisdiction and sector. A closing section covers common concerns in vendor responses.

It was authored by the Australian Signals Directorate's Australian Cyber Security Centre (ASD's ACSC) in partnership with the US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) and National Security Agency (NSA). ASD published it on July 16, 2026, and last reviewed it on November 5, 2026. Its stated audience is small and medium businesses, large organizations and critical infrastructure, and government. It is advisory guidance, not a contractual or regulatory requirement.

## Relationship with other standards

The publication names cyber security leaders, procurement and vendor management teams, and technical stakeholders as its readers. It asks vendors to use algorithms approved by the appropriate authority for the buyer's organization rather than specifying test vectors or protocol detail. Answers are easier to judge against the standards they reference: [FIPS 203](/standards/post-quantum/fips-203/) for key establishment, [FIPS 204](/standards/post-quantum/fips-204/) and [FIPS 205](/standards/post-quantum/fips-205/) for signatures. Ask separately about long-lived verifiers such as firmware and hardware roots of trust, which may not be updatable in the field; see [Firmware and secure boot](/technology-options/firmware-secure-boot/).

The guide complements ASD's [planning publication](/standards/migration-guides/asd-planning/). Pair it with the handbook's [Work with suppliers](/plan/program/suppliers/) practice and the [register](/templates/records/register/), where supplier answers are recorded.
