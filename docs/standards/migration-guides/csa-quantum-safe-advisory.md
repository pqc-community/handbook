## What it covers

The Cyber Security Agency of Singapore (CSA) advisory sets out six steps for organizations starting a post-quantum migration, drawn from CSA's Quantum-Safe Migration Handbook: understand your risk and identify your most important systems; establish clear ownership; identify, evaluate and test replacement algorithms; engage vendors and partners; build awareness across the organization; and build in cryptographic agility. It starts prioritization with sensitive data that must stay confidential for years, internet-facing systems, and long-lived equipment that is hard to update, and it lists the questions to put to vendors about product support, upgrade roadmaps, timelines, responsibility for the migration and cost.

CSA published the advisory on October 14, 2026. It says it "applies broadly to organisations reliant on digital systems, data, or communications for their operations", which makes it one of the few authority publications written for organizations generally rather than for large or critical estates. Owners of Critical Information Infrastructure (CIIOs) are directed instead to CSA's June 2026 guidance for them. The advisory recommends rather than binds: organizations "should aim" to have a migration plan by end 2027, and other organizations exposed to quantum risks "are encouraged" to complete migration by 2035.

## Relationship with other standards

The milestones apply to CSA's audiences in Singapore, with 2031 for CII owners and end 2027 and 2035 for other organizations, rather than serving as universal deadlines; see [Why migration timelines differ](/learn/explainers/why-timelines-differ/). CSA's reason for acting now is its view that a cryptographically relevant quantum computer "could arrive within 5 – 10 years, though the exact timeline remains uncertain".

The advisory points to CSA's Quantum-Safe Migration Handbook for step-by-step planning and its list of recommended algorithms, and to the Quantum Readiness Index, a self-assessment tool for finding capability gaps. Its steps closely match the handbook's [core actions](/plan/core-actions/), and its vendor questions overlap with those in [Work with suppliers](/plan/program/suppliers/). Its sixth step, building in cryptographic agility, corresponds to [Keep the ability to change again](/plan/program/changing-algorithms-again/). For other national migration guidance, see [Post-quantum migration guides](/standards/migration-guides/).
