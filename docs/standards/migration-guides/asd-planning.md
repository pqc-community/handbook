## What it covers

ASD's planning publication helps Australian organisations prepare for the transition away from quantum-vulnerable public-key cryptography. It recommends building a refined transition plan, locating where traditional asymmetric cryptography (RSA, Diffie–Hellman, ECDH and ECDSA) is used, recording it in a cryptographic bill of materials (CBOM), and structuring the work with ASD's LATICE framework: Locate, Assess, Triage, Implement, and Communicate and educate.

The Australian Signals Directorate (ASD) published the guidance on 6 July 2022 and last reviewed it on 22 September 2025. It is written for small and medium businesses, large organisations and critical infrastructure, and government. It is guidance that recommends rather than binds; ASD notes that in its Information Security Manual (ISM) it recommends ceasing the use of traditional asymmetric cryptography by the end of 2030, and that resources from other bodies may differ.

## Relationship with other standards

The guidance is written for ASD's Australian audience, and its milestones apply to that audience rather than serving as a universal deadline; see [Timelines](/learn/explainers/why-timelines-differ/). ASD points organisations to the ASD-approved post-quantum algorithms in the ISM Guidelines for cryptography. ASD states that it "does not recommend, but does not prohibit" post-quantum/traditional (PQ/T) hybrid schemes and expects organisations to move to purely post-quantum cryptography. ASD does not support quantum key distribution (QKD). For how other national authorities position algorithms and hybrids, see [National algorithm positions](/standards/national-algorithm-positions/).

The publication does not provide a supplier questionnaire; pair it with ASD's companion [vendor questions guide](/standards/migration-guides/vendor-questions/) and, for the discovery step, the handbook's [Inventory the cryptography](/programme/inventory/) practice.
