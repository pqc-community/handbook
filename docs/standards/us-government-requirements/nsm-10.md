## What it covers

National Security Memorandum 10 (NSM-10) sets US government policy both to sustain US leadership in quantum computing and to prepare federal systems for the risk a cryptanalytically relevant quantum computer poses to today's public-key cryptography. Section 3(a) sets the goal of mitigating as much quantum risk as feasible by 2035, and the memorandum starts the work that later directives build on: Federal Civilian Executive Branch (FCEB) agencies are to inventory their quantum-vulnerable systems each year and report those inventories to the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) and the National Cyber Director.

The President signed NSM-10 on 4 May 2022. It is presidential direction to US federal departments and agencies; it does not bind private organisations or other governments. The Office of Management and Budget (OMB) inventory requirements it calls for exclude national security systems, which are handled through separate national-security channels (see [CNSA 2.0](/standards/national-algorithm-positions/cnsa-2-0/)).

## Relationship with other standards

NSM-10 is the starting point for US federal post-quantum policy. The [Quantum Computing Cybersecurity Preparedness Act](/standards/us-government-requirements/qccpa/) later put inventories and migration planning into law, and [EO 14412](/standards/us-government-requirements/eo-14412/) and [OMB M-26-15](/standards/us-government-requirements/omb-m-26-15/) brought forward deadlines for the most sensitive federal systems: post-quantum key establishment by 31 December 2030 and post-quantum signatures by 31 December 2031.

[NIST IR 8547 (PDF)](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.IR.8547), October 2026, cites NSM-10 as setting the 2035 target for completing the federal migration. That 2035 date also lines up with IR 8547's plan to disallow quantum-vulnerable public-key algorithms under NIST standards after 2035, but the two are different instruments for different audiences. For how audience-specific timelines differ, see [Timelines](/learn/timelines/).
