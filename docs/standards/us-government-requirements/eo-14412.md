## What it covers

Executive Order 14412, Securing the Nation Against Advanced Cryptographic Attacks, sets out how US federal agencies are to move to post-quantum cryptography and assigns work to OMB, NIST, CISA, NSA and the Federal Acquisition Regulatory (FAR) Council. Its main provisions are:

- each agency was to designate a lead for post-quantum migration within 30 days (by July 22, 2026);
- OMB was to issue guidance within 90 days (by September 20, 2026) requiring high-value assets and high-impact systems to use post-quantum key establishment by December 31, 2030, and post-quantum digital signatures by December 31, 2031 (OMB did so in [M-26-15](/standards/us-government-requirements/omb-m-26-15/));
- NIST is to start a pilot within 180 days, to be completed by December 31, 2027;
- NSA is to report on national security systems within 180 days and annually after that;
- CISA and NIST are to publish guidance on the minimum elements of a cryptographic bill of materials (CBOM) within 270 days;
- NIST is to revise the processes of the Cryptographic Module Validation Program (CMVP) within 180 days to accelerate validations of cryptographic modules (NIST IR 8547 describes this as speeding validation of modules implementing post-quantum algorithms);
- the FAR Council is to propose, within 180 days, a rule requiring federal contractors to comply with NIST FIPS, including post-quantum cryptography, by December 31, 2030;
- the FAR Council is to publish, within 270 days, a proposed rule requiring contractors' vulnerability disclosure programs to accept reports of cryptographic weaknesses.

The President signed EO 14412 on June 22, 2026, and it was published in the Federal Register on June 25, 2026 (91 FR 38483). It is presidential direction to US federal agencies; national security systems are covered through NSA. It does not itself place obligations on contractors: the 2030 contractor requirement exists only as a rule the order tells the FAR Council to propose, and it binds contractors only if and when a final FAR rule takes effect. Critical infrastructure owners and operators are engaged through Sector Risk Management Agencies and CISA rather than bound directly.

## Relationship with other standards

EO 14412 builds on [NSM-10](/standards/us-government-requirements/nsm-10/) and [EO 14306](/standards/us-government-requirements/eo-14306/), and is carried out for agencies by [OMB M-26-15](/standards/us-government-requirements/omb-m-26-15/). The order gives key establishment (2030) and signatures (2031) separate dates. Their drivers differ: traffic recorded today can be decrypted later, whereas a forged signature becomes possible only once a capable quantum computer exists. Long-lived roots of trust and verifiers that cannot easily be updated need planning well before the signature date; see [Firmware and secure boot](/technology-options/firmware-secure-boot/).

[NIST IR 8547 (PDF)](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.IR.8547), October 2026, was issued in support of EO 14412 and notes that, as the order directs, NIST is revising [FIPS 140-3](/standards/modules-protocols-certificates/fips-140-3/) CMVP processes to accelerate validation of modules that implement post-quantum algorithms. The CBOM guidance the order calls for relates to [inventory work](/plan/program/inventory/); suppliers to the US government should watch the FAR rulemaking rather than treat 2030 as a contract term already in force.
