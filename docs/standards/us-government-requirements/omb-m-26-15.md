## What it covers

OMB Memorandum M-26-15 tells US federal agencies how to carry out their move to post-quantum cryptography. It implements [EO 14412](/standards/us-government-requirements/eo-14412/) and Section 4(c) of the [Quantum Computing Cybersecurity Preparedness Act](/standards/us-government-requirements/qccpa/) (Public Law 117-260). Its objective is to mitigate as much quantum risk as feasible for priority systems by 31 December 2030; priority systems are high-impact systems, high-value assets and systems holding highly sensitive information. Its main provisions are:

- each agency is to send a PQC Migration Plan, aligned with [NIST IR 8547](/standards/transition-and-key-management/nist-ir-8547/), to OMB and the Office of the National Cyber Director (ONCD) within 120 days;
- the memorandum sets out phases agencies should follow: discovery in 2026–27; pilots and early migration in 2027–28; post-quantum key establishment in 2028–30; signature migration in 2031; and, by 2035, completing the migration of remaining systems based on risk assessment and the availability of commercial offerings;
- the General Services Administration (GSA) is to set up a Federal Identity, Credential, and Access Management (FICAM) working group within 60 days;
- it refers to CISA's list of product categories in which post-quantum products are widely available;
- Appendix A requires agencies to support TLS 1.3 by 2 January 2030.

OMB issued M-26-15 on 24 June 2026, addressed to the heads of executive departments and agencies. It is binding direction for those agencies and states that it "does not apply to national security systems", which follow NSA requirements such as [CNSA 2.0](/standards/national-algorithm-positions/cnsa-2-0/). It is not addressed to contractors, other governments or private organisations, though its phasing is a useful reference for any organisation's plan.

## Relationship with other standards

M-26-15 is where federal policy turns into agency work. It is the OMB guidance [EO 14412](/standards/us-government-requirements/eo-14412/) directed; the firm 2030 key-establishment and 2031 signature deadlines come from the executive order, and the memorandum sets out the phases agencies should follow to meet them; it repeats the TLS 1.3 deadline from [EO 14306](/standards/us-government-requirements/eo-14306/); it implements Section 4(c) of the [Quantum Computing Cybersecurity Preparedness Act](/standards/us-government-requirements/qccpa/); and its 2035 phase matches the [NSM-10](/standards/us-government-requirements/nsm-10/) goal. It also refers to the [CISA PQC product categories](/standards/us-government-requirements/cisa-pqc-product-categories/).

[NIST IR 8547 (PDF)](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.IR.8547), October 2026, sets general deprecation of quantum-vulnerable public-key algorithms after 2030 and disallowance after 2035, and notes that M-26-15 sets earlier deadlines for high-value assets and high-impact systems. IR 8547 also notes that M-26-15 directs FICAM modernisation in support of post-quantum cryptography, and describes moving critical trust infrastructure such as [FIPS 201-3](/standards/modules-protocols-certificates/fips-201-3/) identity credentials as a significant, sustained effort. That signature-heavy, long-lived infrastructure needs planning well before the 2031 signature phase. See [Decide what to migrate first](/programme/priority/).
