## What it covers

NIST SP 800-185 defines four functions built on the Keccak permutation from [FIPS 202](/standards/symmetric-and-hash/fips-202/): cSHAKE (a customizable XOF that supports domain separation), KMAC (a keyed message authentication code with variable-length output), TupleHash (unambiguous hashing of a sequence of strings) and ParallelHash. KMAC is the SHA-3-based counterpart to HMAC and can be used as the pseudorandom function in NIST key-derivation methods.

NIST issues Special Publications in the 800 series as guidance and recommendations for US federal agencies; they are widely used as a reference by others. An SP is not itself mandatory unless a FIPS, directive or contract makes it so.

## Relationship with other standards

[NIST IR 8547](/standards/transition-and-key-management/nist-ir-8547/) (October 2026) cites SP 800-185 among NIST's XOF standards and says NIST does not expect to need to transition away from its symmetric standards as part of the post-quantum migration. The Rev. 1 draft is ordinary maintenance of the publication, not a post-quantum replacement; teams relying on cSHAKE or KMAC should track the final text.

KMAC appears as a pseudorandom function option in [NIST SP 800-108](/standards/symmetric-and-hash/sp-800-108/) and [NIST SP 800-56C](/standards/symmetric-and-hash/sp-800-56c/), and [NIST SP 800-227](/standards/post-quantum/sp-800-227/) discusses how shared secrets from key encapsulation are combined and derived.
