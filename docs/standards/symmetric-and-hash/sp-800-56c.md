## What it covers

NIST SP 800-56C specifies one-step and two-step (extract-then-expand) methods for deriving keying material from a shared secret produced by key establishment, using hash functions, HMAC or KMAC. It is the step between "two parties now share a secret" and "they hold the session keys a protocol actually uses".

NIST issues Special Publications in the 800 series as guidance and recommendations for US federal agencies; they are widely used as a reference by others. An SP is not itself mandatory unless a FIPS, directive or contract makes it so.

## Relationship with other standards

[NIST IR 8547](/standards/transition-and-key-management/nist-ir-8547/) (October 2026) cites SP 800-56C among NIST's key-derivation standards that it does not expect to need to replace. It also gives SP 800-56C a direct role in hybrid key establishment. Section 3.2.1 says NIST currently allows a generic composite technique described in SP 800-56C: a shared secret Z from [NIST SP 800-56A](/standards/classical-public-key/sp-800-56a/) or [NIST SP 800-56B](/standards/classical-public-key/sp-800-56b/) is concatenated with a second shared secret T (Z' = Z || T), and the result is treated as the shared secret for SP 800-56C key derivation. The technique is allowed even if T comes from an unapproved source, including a disallowed algorithm.

IR 8547 says NIST intends to update SP 800-56C so that Z may be generated by any current or future NIST key-establishment standard, including [FIPS 203](/standards/post-quantum/fips-203/), and so that the order may be reversed (T || Z); the protocol must fix the order. It also emphasizes that a FIPS 203 shared secret key may already be used as Z. Combiner guidance is in [NIST SP 800-227](/standards/post-quantum/sp-800-227/). This concerns key establishment only; hybrid or dual signatures are a separate topic covered on [Hybrid approaches](/learn/hybrid/).
