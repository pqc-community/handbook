## What it covers

FIPS 180-4 specifies SHA-1 and the SHA-2 family of hash functions: SHA-224, SHA-256, SHA-384, SHA-512, SHA-512/224 and SHA-512/256. SHA-2 digests appear throughout signatures, certificates, message authentication, key derivation and the hash-based signature schemes, so the standard is in scope for almost any cryptographic inventory.

FIPS 180-4 is issued by NIST. NIST's [FIPS compliance FAQ](https://www.nist.gov/standardsgov/compliance-faqs-federal-information-processing-standards-fips) (content last updated 3 July 2024) describes FIPS as standards for federal computer systems that are not always mandatory for federal agencies (each FIPS's applicability section says when it is), notes that FIPS do not apply to national security systems, and says private-sector organisations use them voluntarily, although companies with government contracts must comply with FISMA, which mandates the use of FIPS.

## Relationship with other standards

[NIST IR 8547](/standards/transition-and-key-management/nist-ir-8547/) (October 2026) says NIST does not expect to need to transition away from its symmetric standards, including hash functions. Its Table 8 gives collision security categories of 2 for SHA-256 and SHA-512/256, 4 for SHA-384 and 5 for SHA-512. SHA-224 and SHA-512/224 have 112-bit classical collision strength, which IR 8547 places below category 1 for collision resistance (their preimage resistance is category 3). IR 8547 states that NIST's 112-bit symmetric standards will be disallowed in 2030 and refers to [NIST SP 800-131A](/standards/transition-and-key-management/sp-800-131a/).

SHA-1 is a separate case. IR 8547 (footnote 4) says SHA-1 is currently deprecated for all remaining technical applications and will be disallowed for use in FIPS-validated cryptographic modules after 2030, consistent with NIST's December 2022 announcement. Removing SHA-1 is a classical clean-up, not a post-quantum change, but it often lands in the same certificate and signing work.

For US national security systems, the NSA's CNSA 2.0 FAQ (October 2026) specifies SHA-384 or SHA-512; see [CNSA 2.0](/standards/national-algorithm-positions/cnsa-2-0/). SHA-2 is also the hash inside SHA-2 parameter sets of [FIPS 205](/standards/post-quantum/fips-205/) and [NIST SP 800-208](/standards/post-quantum/sp-800-208/).
