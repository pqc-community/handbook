## What it covers

NIST SP 800-90A specifies the approved deterministic random bit generators (DRBGs) Hash_DRBG, HMAC_DRBG and CTR_DRBG, which, seeded from an entropy source, produce the random values used for key generation, nonces and the randomness inside post-quantum algorithms.

NIST issues Special Publications in the 800 series as guidance and recommendations for US federal agencies; they are widely used as a reference by others. An SP is not itself mandatory unless a FIPS, directive or contract makes it so.

## Relationship with other standards

[NIST IR 8547](/standards/transition-and-key-management/nist-ir-8547/) (October 2026) cites SP 800-90A among NIST's DRBG standards and says NIST does not expect to need to transition away from its symmetric standards as part of the post-quantum migration. The proposed SHAKE-based generator would add a DRBG option built on [FIPS 202](/standards/symmetric-and-hash/fips-202/).

Random bit generation matters for the new algorithms. A note to IR 8547's Table 3 says ML-DSA-44 ([FIPS 204](/standards/post-quantum/fips-204/)) drops from category 2 to category 1 if used with an approved random bit generator of less than 192 bits of security strength.
