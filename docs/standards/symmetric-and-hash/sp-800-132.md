## What it covers

NIST SP 800-132 specifies password-based key derivation (PBKDF2), which uses an approved pseudorandom function such as HMAC with a salt and an iteration count to derive keys from a password for protecting stored data. Its strength depends mainly on the password and the parameters chosen, not on any public-key algorithm.

NIST issues Special Publications in the 800 series as guidance and recommendations for US federal agencies; they are widely used as a reference by others. An SP is not itself mandatory unless a FIPS, directive or contract makes it so.

## Relationship with other standards

[NIST IR 8547](/standards/transition-and-key-management/nist-ir-8547/) (October 2026) cites SP 800-132 among NIST's key-derivation standards and says NIST does not expect to need to transition away from its symmetric standards as part of the post-quantum migration. Password-derived keys are not a post-quantum migration item in themselves; their protection still rests on password quality and derivation parameters.

The functions PBKDF2 relies on are in [FIPS 198-1](/standards/symmetric-and-hash/fips-198-1/) and [FIPS 180-4](/standards/symmetric-and-hash/fips-180-4/). Key derivation from established keys is in [NIST SP 800-108](/standards/symmetric-and-hash/sp-800-108/).
