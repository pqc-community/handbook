## What it covers

NIST Special Publication (SP) 800-56A specifies pair-wise key-agreement schemes based on the discrete logarithm problem over finite fields and elliptic curves, including Diffie-Hellman (DH), Elliptic Curve Diffie-Hellman (ECDH) and Menezes-Qu-Vanstone (MQV). In these schemes both parties contribute to the shared secret, so neither can determine it alone. SP 800-56A covers key establishment only; signatures are a separate job covered by [FIPS 186-5](/standards/classical-public-key/fips-186-5/).

NIST issues SPs as guidance and recommendations for US federal agencies; others widely use them as a reference. The current version is Revision 3. In January 2026 NIST said it had decided to update the publication; no Revision 4 draft is shown on NIST's CSRC page.

## Relationship with other standards

[NIST IR 8547](/standards/transition-and-key-management/nist-ir-8547/), final October 2026, lists the SP 800-56A schemes as quantum-vulnerable and names [FIPS 203](/standards/post-quantum/fips-203/) (ML-KEM) as the only approved post-quantum key-establishment scheme at the time of the report. Because of harvest-now, decrypt-later risk, IR 8547 notes that application-specific guidance may require or recommend migration before 2035 (§4.1.2). For US federal high value assets and high-impact systems, [OMB M-26-15](/standards/us-government-requirements/omb-m-26-15/) requires quantum-resistant key establishment by 31 December 2030. Other authorities set their own dates; see [Why migration timelines differ](/learn/explainers/why-timelines-differ/).

SP 800-56A also has a transitional role in hybrid key establishment. IR 8547 describes the generic composite technique in [SP 800-56C](/standards/symmetric-and-hash/sp-800-56c/): a shared secret Z from SP 800-56A or SP 800-56B is concatenated with a second shared secret T, and the result is fed to an SP 800-56C key derivation method. The technique is allowed even if T comes from an unapproved or disallowed algorithm, and the combination can be FIPS 140-validated as long as at least one input comes from an approved scheme (§3.2.1, §4.1.2). Further guidance on combiners is in [SP 800-227](/standards/post-quantum/sp-800-227/).
