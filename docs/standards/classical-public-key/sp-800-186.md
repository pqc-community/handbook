## What it covers

NIST Special Publication (SP) 800-186 specifies the elliptic curves NIST recommends for use with its classical public-key standards. These include prime-field Weierstrass curves (such as P-256, P-384 and P-521), Montgomery curves (Curve25519 and Curve448) and twisted Edwards curves (Edwards25519 and Edwards448). The curves are used by two different jobs: digital signatures in [FIPS 186-5](/standards/classical-public-key/fips-186-5/) and elliptic-curve key establishment in [NIST SP 800-56A](/standards/classical-public-key/sp-800-56a/).

NIST issues SPs as guidance and recommendations for US federal agencies; others widely use them as a reference. SP 800-186 was published alongside FIPS 186-5.

## Relationship with other standards

[NIST IR 8547](/standards/transition-and-key-management/nist-ir-8547/), final October 2026, identifies the elliptic-curve schemes that use these curves, in FIPS 186-5 and SP 800-56A, as vulnerable to Shor's algorithm on a cryptanalytically relevant quantum computer. It names [FIPS 203](/standards/post-quantum/fips-203/) for key establishment and [FIPS 204](/standards/post-quantum/fips-204/), [FIPS 205](/standards/post-quantum/fips-205/) and [SP 800-208](/standards/post-quantum/sp-800-208/) as the post-quantum standards for signatures.

During the transition, these curves can appear as the classical component of a hybrid. For key establishment, IR 8547 describes the SP 800-56C generic composite, in which an elliptic-curve shared secret can be combined with a post-quantum one (§3.2.1); for signatures, it accommodates dual signatures where at least one component is NIST-approved (§3.2.2). See [Hybrid cryptography](/learn/hybrid/).
