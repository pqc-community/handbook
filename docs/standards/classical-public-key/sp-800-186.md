## What it covers

NIST SP 800-186 defines NIST-recommended elliptic curves — including prime-field Weierstrass curves (such as P-256, P-384, and P-521), Montgomery curves (Curve25519 and Curve448), and twisted Edwards curves (Edwards25519 and Edwards448) — used by digital signatures in [FIPS 186-5](/standards/classical-public-key/fips-186-5/) and key-establishment schemes in [NIST SP 800-56A](/standards/classical-public-key/sp-800-56a/).

:::info[Official texts]

- **Authority:** National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)
- **Publication:** NIST Special Publication 800-186 (2023)
- **Status:** Final Special Publication (classical, quantum-vulnerable)
- **Official text:** [NIST publication page via DOI (HTML)](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.SP.800-186)

:::

## Relationship with other standards

[NIST IR 8547](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.IR.8547), October 2026, explains that elliptic-curve cryptography relies on the elliptic-curve discrete logarithm problem, which is vulnerable to Shor's algorithm regardless of curve choice. During transition, curves from SP 800-186 continue to appear as the classical component inside hybrid key-exchange or dual-signature deployments while systems migrate to [FIPS 203](/standards/post-quantum/fips-203/) and [FIPS 204](/standards/post-quantum/fips-204/).
